A citation check for AI-written research.

Courts have logged 1,116 US filings with AI-fabricated content (1,600 worldwide). Research is drafted by the same models. Here, every figure is checked against the SEC filing by a Chainlink CRE workflow, a wrong claim costs its author, and a trading agent can only act on what held.

The legal gap

Research rules assume a person. AI drafts research now, and cannot certify a belief.

Regulation AC · 17 CFR 242.501(a)(1)a certification "attesting that all of the views expressed in the research report accurately reflect the research analyst's personal views". The analyst: "any natural person…" (242.500)
FINRA Rule 2241(c)(1)(A)procedures "reasonably designed to ensure that purported facts in its research reports are based on reliable information"
FINRA Regulatory Notice 24-09FINRA's rules "are intended to be technology neutral" and "continue to apply when member firms use Gen AI"
FINRA 2026 oversight report · AI agentsconsider "how to track agent actions and decisions; or how to establish guardrails"

Courts already know the failure: 1,116 US court filings with AI-fabricated content, 1,600 worldwide (Charlotin database, reported by AFP, June 2026).

How it works

A record of what was said, what the filing says, and what was done about it.

01

Stake

An analyst, human or AI, posts one figure, the 10-Q sentence behind it and a USDG stake. The exact words go on-chain.

02

Check

A Chainlink CRE workflow fetches the filing from SEC EDGAR and runs the check (on a DON, on every node). The contract has no verifier key.

03

Pay

Wrong: the stake goes to a readers' fund fixed at deploy, in the same transaction as the verdict; each payee withdraws its share (claimReaderShare). Right: it comes back.

04

Gate

A trading agent cannot cite research that failed. The trade reverts, naming the claim.

05

Journal

Each trade keeps the claims it cites, the verdict on each and the agent's reasoning; the record's hash is on-chain.

Chainlink Runtime Environment

The check is a Chainlink CRE workflow. The contract has no verifier key.

Posted event → EVM log trigger → HTTP: SEC EDGAR (gzip, ~100 KB) → the check (on a DON, on every node) → identical consensus → report → forwarder → BondedClaims.onReport: owner, name, pinned workflow id, claim hash
3,249claims on five real 10-Qs: same verdict, reason and score as the Python check, on Bun and on QuickJS (the engine CRE runs)
50/50verdicts in a local round built by the workflow's own handler in Chainlink's SDK test runtime, each matching the Python check
0verifier keys in the contract. On a DON only signed reports land; one compiled workflow id can be pinned, forever

Run: WASM build (cre workflow build), the SDK test runtime, QuickJS parity. Not yet: the CRE CLI simulation on testnet (pending login), a DON. In simulation, Chainlink's mock forwarder takes anyone's report.

The reasoning trail

Every trade keeps its reasons. Re-verify redoes them in the browser.

News Checker (RYO): the same agent reads posts by market voices, finds each figure in the 10-Q and checks the story as a staked claim. A loud post with the wrong quarter moves nothing. Demo posts: 20, written fixtures by fictional accounts. Decisions: fixed rules by default (re-run in the browser); optionally a model behind an OpenAI-compatible endpoint, its exact reply hashed into the record (tested, not used in the demo round).

For lawyers

What the record proves, and what it does not.

It proves

  • the exact words of each claim, who staked on them, when
  • which filing bytes the verdict was checked against, and by which workflow
  • that a trade cited only claims that had cleared
  • that its stated reasons were fixed when it was made

It does not prove

  • that a view was honestly held (Reg AC's certification)
  • a reasonable basis for a rating or target (2241(c)(1)(B))
  • that the filing is true, or the claim complete
  • who is behind a wallet, or Rule 17a-4 compliance

Trust, stated plainly

What is trusted today, and what is next.

Today

  • Simulation writes through Chainlink's mock forwarder: anyone can settle there. It proves the path, not the security
  • The filing is what SEC EDGAR serves; its hash (less the CDN's script tag) is on-chain
  • The check says what it cannot do: a limit versus a balance
  • On the test market the deployer stands in for the readers' fund; who it is in production is a legal choice

Next

  • A live DON, the KeystoneForwarder, a pinned workflow id (after SEC's User-Agent rule is solved)
  • Robinhood Chain mainnet, bonded in USDG
  • 8-Ks and 10-Ks; one compliance team piloting cleared-only publication
