## Name

Vouch

## Tagline (under 80 characters)

A citation check for AI-written financial research, run by Chainlink CRE.

## One-liner

Courts keep catching AI-invented citations in legal briefs; AI-drafted
investment research has the same failure. Vouch checks every figure an AI
analyst cites against the SEC filing, through a Chainlink CRE workflow, and
keeps a record a compliance officer can show an examiner.

## Short description (about 60 words)

By June 2026 a public database had logged about 1,600 court filings worldwide
with AI-fabricated content, 1,116 of them in the US. Research is drafted by the
same models. Vouch makes each cited figure accountable: the analyst stakes
USDG, a Chainlink CRE workflow checks the figure against the SEC filing, a
wrong claim loses its stake, and trading agents can act only on what held.

## Description

**The problem lawyers already know.** Lawyers have been sanctioned for filing
briefs with cases an AI made up. Damien Charlotin's database had 1,600 such
court filings from 35 countries by June 2026, 1,116 of them in the US (AFP,
June 2026). Investment research is now drafted by the same models, with the
same failure: "$274 million for Q2" from a sentence where $274 million is the
six-month figure. The rules are written for people. Regulation AC has a
"natural person" certify the views; FINRA Rule 2241(c)(1)(A) requires that
"purported facts in its research reports are based on reliable information";
FINRA says firms "are responsible for their communications, regardless of
whether they are generated by a human or AI". A model cannot certify a belief.
A firm can show that every number was checked.

**What Vouch does.**

1. **Stake.** An analyst, human or AI, posts a claim: one figure, the filing
   sentence that supports it, and the filing's accession number, with a USDG
   stake. The exact words go on-chain.
2. **Check.** The claim's on-chain event triggers a Chainlink CRE workflow. It
   fetches the filing from SEC EDGAR and runs a deterministic check: the quote
   must be a whole sentence of the filing, every figure exact, and the period,
   quarter, date and direction as filed.
3. **Pay.** A wrong claim loses its stake in the same transaction as the
   verdict, which also records the hash of the exact filing bytes checked. The
   stake goes to a readers' fund whose payees are fixed when the market is
   deployed; each withdraws its own share. Nobody can redirect it later.
4. **Gate.** An AI trading agent trades through `AgentDesk`, which refuses,
   on-chain, any trade citing a claim that failed or was never checked.
5. **Record.** Every paper trade keeps the claims it relied on, each verdict,
   the agent's reasoning and its refusals, hashed on-chain. The page's
   **Download the audit record** button gives one file per trade, with the
   steps to re-check it from the filing.

**Why stake, if the check is public?** Because the stake turns self-checking
into a commitment. An AI that checks before it publishes costs nothing; one
that publishes without checking pays. That is the behaviour a compliance
officer wants. For a regulated firm the stake is optional: the check and the
record are the compliance product, and the bond is for open AI analysts and
newsletters that want a public track record.

**Why lawyers should care.** It is a control a firm can show an examiner for
2241(c)(1)(A): every number checked against the issuer's own filing, by a
process the firm does not control, on a public record anyone can re-run. It is
the trace and guardrail FINRA's 2026 oversight report asks for when firms use
AI agents ("how to track agent actions and decisions"). It does not prove a
view was honestly held, that a recommendation had a reasonable basis, that the
filing is true, or that a record meets Rule 17a-4. The one-page brief is
[docs/bli/LEGAL.md](LEGAL.md).

**For RYO-CHAN: a news checker with a reasoning trail.** The agent reads
real public posts about the five companies (130 posts from Mastodon's public
tag timelines, July to September 2026, snapshot and hashes in the repo). A
language model reads each one for ticker, figure, period, sentiment,
conviction and urgency, and says whether the figure is one the 10-Q reports;
the exact messages, the model id and its exact reply are hashed into the
record, so Re-verify can check the reply gives exactly the reading. The agent
then stakes on each story itself, but only where a sentence of the filing
states the figure: the check reads sentences, so a figure the filing gives
only in a table can be neither confirmed nor refuted, and the agent does not
bet on its own blind spot. What held moves it; a refused claim cannot. It
decides by fixed written rules (or, with `VOUCH_DECIDER=model`, a model whose
reply is hashed the same way), and every paper trade keeps its reasoning
on-chain. In the run with Claude Haiku 4.5 as the reader, 99 posts went to
the model, 16 stated a checkable figure, 3 had a filing sentence, and all 3
held. With the word-list reader instead, one real post's stale figure ("$1.5B
in 3 months", Palantir's six-month 2025 gross profit) was caught and slashed.

**For Chainlink: CRE is the orchestration layer.** EVM log trigger on
Robinhood Chain → HTTP capability to SEC EDGAR → deterministic check →
identical-consensus aggregation → report → forwarder → `BondedClaims.onReport`,
which accepts a verdict only from the forwarder and, on a production market,
only from the configured workflow owner and name and a pinned workflow id. The
check is a line-for-line port of the Python check; parity tests give the same
verdict, reason and score on 3,249 claims about five real 10-Qs, on Bun and on
QuickJS (the engine CRE runs). One script runs the CLI simulation per claim
(`scripts/cre_round.sh`).
